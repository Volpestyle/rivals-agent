# Separate calibration actuation from measurement, 2026-09-28

Evaluator recommendation to herdr-lead, following James's question about guard cost. Advisory; no live authorization. This supersedes pursuing the startup-retry candidate as the next calibration change. Its diagnosis and detach work remain useful.

## Decision

**Yes: use a fixed, camera-only schedule and measure its outcome offline. Pause the startup-retry delta.** The live tool currently makes adequate measurement evidence a prerequisite for generating the very motion we need to measure. Pose NCC, low texture, Spider-Sense distortion and prime-response confidence can reject a calibration interval without ending a fixed right-stick experiment. A half-second image gap does not change the requested right-stick schedule. The growing recovery machinery is the wrong next investment.

This is a real instance of the same failure pattern as the billing watchdog: an observer's uncertainty terminates otherwise useful work. The corrective principle is to put each check at the boundary it protects. It does not imply removing the input limits or borrowing this exception for a feedback policy whose actions depend on the latest image.

One factual correction matters. Not every previous stop was a measurement-quality stop. Sitting c's first yaw attempt had an idle warning and the game subsequently dropped to the lobby; sitting e's yaw-02 also refused an idle-warning view before attachment. Sitting d's post-attach clause remains unknown. Pose and motion checks caused avoidable execution coupling, but the records do not justify saying no scope-related stop occurred.

## What remains live

- **A right-stick-only command boundary.** No buttons, triggers or left stick on any report, including priming and cleanup. Predeclare finite axis values, signs, durations, neutral gaps, total attached time and maximum report count/time. A malformed or out-of-budget schedule refuses before attachment. A delayed command is skipped or ends the block; it never starts a fresh full-duration hold after its deadline, catches up, or automatically repeats.
- **Focus, human takeover and scope.** Check a fresh range frame before attaching and retain a lightweight range/idle monitor while attached. Stop for a failed range/idle check; a pre-attach-only check cannot detect an inactivity drop. Check mouse buttons as well as the existing keyboard poll: `any_key_pressed()` explicitly excludes mouse buttons, and sitting e retained a 904-second held attack. James refreshes movement/attack, releases input and hands over immediately before the bounded run. The calibration executor never adds its own attack or navigation step.
- **A short, explicit capture-blackout allowance for that monitor.** The current 100 ms limit conflates image feedback age with permission for a predetermined camera pulse. For this calibration-only path, I propose reviewing a one-second maximum age of the last positive range check, independently of frame quality, and immediate termination on a returned frame that fails the range/idle check. The approximately 483 ms observed stall fits; an indefinitely blind run does not. One second is a proposed operational bound, not a measured universal safe value. The input lease remains short and is renewed by the local schedule/scope monitor, not by a timestamp manufactured to look like a fresh frame. This is a specific change to the repository's fresh-frame-per-input contract and needs explicit lead disposition and pre-run review. Keep the learned-controller contract unchanged.
- **Independent release and owned-device removal.** A local deadline/deadman must still release and detach if capture, logging, the scheduler or its remote client stalls. Reuse the owned-target removal work, with no later writes, at-most-once remove and exactly-once free. If native removal fails, record that and end the owning pad process promptly; do not kill the game. Neither neutral nor garbage collection proves unplug. Do not promise guaranteed hardware detach: the binding documents process-exit cleanup, but a native call holding the actuator lock can block a thread-based closer. Review that failure path explicitly.

These checks are small compared with live motion estimation. They protect the allowed input and session, rather than adjudicating whether the video will yield a usable calibration.

Completing a script is not a camera calibration. If the complete recording has too little usable evidence, use that recording to choose the next view or estimator change; do not resume patching live quality guards or run an automatic repeat.
