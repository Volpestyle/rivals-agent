## One bounded audit

**Question:** In quiet-camera contexts, can an observer identify a visually supported focus target before the turn, and does using that target explain native yaw better than the nearest-bot proxy? Distinguish wrong target identity from absent/ambiguous intent and from disagreement about the timing of an otherwise sensible turn.

1. Use only the same eight admitted TRAIN sessions in the completed probe, through the existing roster/denylist checks. Retain its exact seven-bin target, quiet-history rule and 0.4630332-degree threshold. No new dev selection, sealed access, label recalibration or detector extraction. Every session has ample recorded onset/still support.
2. Select **three onset clusters and three quiet/still controls per session: 24 + 24 = 48 examples**. Use the first eligible anchor of each selected positive cluster; select three distinct quiet/still blocks and one random eligible anchor per block. Fixed seed 29, at least two seconds between selected anchors, without ranking by model errors or inspecting pixels. Keep shortages as shortages. Balance/order is hidden from the annotator; randomize all examples together. This deliberately balanced sample measures case types, not population onset precision or F1.
3. **Causal pass:** show only the eight source frames available to the probe, ending at the anchor, at native resolution with zoom. Hide later frames, mouse/action traces, predictions and onset/control membership. Record visible candidate bot IDs/boxes, nearest-to-reticle ID, whether one focus target is supported by the visible interaction (or multiple/none/unknown), its normalized horizontal position, and an optional left/none/right prediction with abstention. Mere visibility or proximity does not establish intent. Save these annotations before revealing outcomes; never backfill a causal target from hindsight.
4. **Outcome pass:** reveal up to one second of following native video, still hiding mouse traces and model predictions until visual annotation is complete. Record an eventual target only when an independently visible attack/contact/damage cue identifies a particular bot; screen centering or yaw direction alone is insufficient. Otherwise retain unknown. Link it back to a pre-anchor bot ID only if identity is defensible. Tag visible acquisition/continued engagement, multiple-target choice, traversal/reorientation, or unknown; categories may overlap. This is owner interpretation of observable behavior, not a ground-truth read of James's intention.
5. Unblind native yaw. On exactly the same supported onset cases, compare the horizontal side of the causal focus target with the nearest visible bot's side and the actual turn sign. Use a fixed center band of 2% of frame width as an abstention, with no tuning. Separately report the eventual-target comparison as **hindsight/oracle**. On still controls, report how often the same causal cues would wrongly suggest starting a turn. Report all unknowns/abstentions and paired wins/losses, by session; retain example IDs, annotations and contact sheets. No focal conversion is needed for normalized side/sign.

## Decision this can change

- **Causal target identity helps:** multiple sessions contain clear pre-turn focus targets; choosing that identity fixes paired nearest-bot errors without making the same cue common in still controls. This would justify proposing a target-identity supervision experiment within the end-to-end policy. It does not authorize a fit or a nearest-bot aiming controller.
- **Only hindsight helps:** the eventual target explains turn direction but was not identifiable in the causal pass. The missing piece is an available goal/target-selection signal, or longer causal context, not a demonstrated yaw-head fix. Do not feed the future target into a policy and call it deployable. Keep fits parked until a concrete causal conditioning source is proposed.
- **Support is sparse or neither helps:** record that the audit was inconclusive or negative and stop. Keep policy fits parked while the lead advances calibration and the IDM's own qualification work. More IDM labels are not, by themselves, a remedy for ambiguous goals.

This small TRAIN audit is a design diagnostic, not a statistical acceptance gate or a claim of unseen-session generalization. Even a favorable finding needs a separately authorized matched model comparison. It can run before calibration: pad degrees-per-second does not answer which target a human selected, and the native labels already support this sign comparison. Calibration remains necessary for live execution; IDM qualification remains necessary for expanding trustworthy supervision. Neither is made dependent on this audit.
