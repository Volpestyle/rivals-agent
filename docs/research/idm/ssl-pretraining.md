# Can the unlabelled footage help before Gate 2?

2026-09-26 · research-methods → steering lead, idm-owner and Policy team

**Recommendation: conditional go for a bounded 10-hour source pilot; no-go for a full 50-hour backbone-pretraining campaign now.** Self-supervised learning can use admitted video without assigning actions, so it need not wait for the IDM's Gate 2 labelling result. Source admission, overlap exclusion and current-patch downstream evaluation still apply. The footage is a plausible representation-learning resource, not 50 hours of usable action demonstrations by default.

The cheapest informative first arm is **masked/predictive temporal-feature learning over cached, frozen DINOv2-small features**, followed by paired-data training. It changes the temporal encoder, not the frame encoder. Compare it against exactly the same architecture with a randomly initialized temporal encoder and the same frozen generic DINOv2. This isolates what unlabelled pretraining contributes. Continued frame-encoder training or a small VideoMAE is a subsequent experiment if this pilot or its diagnostics justify the extra cost.

No cited result establishes that 10–50 hours of camera-heavy Rivals footage improves both our IDM and our policy. The closest evidence combines small-label robotic imitation, game latent-action learning and action-recognition SSL. Their tasks and scales differ materially. **An IDM gain would not establish a policy gain**, and lower self-supervised loss would establish neither.

This handoff is research only. No media, compression-log payload, checkpoint, or paired-session payload was opened; no frame was decoded. No training, Mac/cloud job, Linear write, commit, or edit outside this file was performed. Timing numbers below are estimates, not M5 Max benchmarks. Literature results are attributed to the cited paper versions; they are not local measurements.

## 1. What the primary evidence actually shows

Effects below are percentage-point differences unless stated otherwise. Recognition accuracy, partial-credit robot scores and game success are different quantities and should not be compared as one common effect size.

| Method and primary source | Data and labelled scale | Relevant result | What it supports here—and does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| [R3M](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.12601v3), §§3–4, Appendix A | Human Ego4D clips with language annotations; parent dataset has >3,500 hours, not a verified claim that every hour entered every fit. Downstream simulation uses 5–25 demonstrations on two suites and 25–100 on Adroit; real robot uses 20 demonstrations. | Authors report >20% improvement over scratch and >10% over CLIP/MoCo across their simulated suite; their percentage wording is retained here. Real-world mean success exceeds 50%, nearly twice CLIP, with <10 minutes of demonstrations. | Strong small-label BC evidence for video-derived visual representations. It includes language alignment and temporal contrastive learning; it is neither pure video-only SSL nor continued in-domain Rivals pretraining. |
| [VideoMAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.12602v3), Tables 2–4 and Figure 4 | Unlabelled training videos from the same recognition dataset; examples include HMDB51's 3.5k clips and SSv2's 169k. | HMDB51: 62.6% versus scratch 18.0% and MoCo v3 39.2%. SSv2: 69.6% versus 32.6% and 54.2%. A 42k in-domain pretraining subset reaches 68.7% versus 68.5% after 240k Kinetics clips. | Direct evidence that domain and objective matter, and small datasets can work. These are action-recognition results after supervised fine-tuning, not small-label IDM/BC. The 0.2-point domain comparison is modest, not a promised large gain. |
| [VideoMAE v2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16727) and [authors' CVPR slides](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/media/cvpr-2023/Slides/21454.pdf) | 1.35M unlabelled clips; largest encoder about 1B parameters; progressive recipe also uses a labelled post-pretraining dataset. | Reported endpoints include 90.0% K400 and 77.0% SSv2. Dual encoder/decoder masking reduces work. | Useful efficiency ideas, but these endpoints are not a controlled estimate of the benefit of adding 50 unlabelled hours. Do not reproduce the giant model or attribute the labelled stage's benefit to SSL. |
| [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1), Tables 6–7 | 2M pretraining videos; ViT-H about 630M parameters. At 5% SSv2 labels, roughly 48 labelled clips/class. | Frozen H/16: 51.9±.3% versus VideoMAE H/16 41.4±.8% at 5% labels: +10.5 points. Full-label frozen evaluation: 71.4% versus generic DINOv2-g 50.6%, with different architectures/data. | Good evidence for label-efficient motion representations. Not an equal-compute small-model adaptation trial and not action recovery. |
| [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1), §§2–4 | >1M hours of initial video; encoder up to 1B parameters. Robot post-training uses <62 hours **with end-effector states and derived actions**. | Two-lab mean pick/place is 80% for cup and 65% for box versus Octo's 15% and 10%; ten trials per task/lab. | Demonstrates action-conditioned planning transfer after very large pretraining. “Unlabelled” there means no task/reward/success labels, not no action information. It is not evidence that our input-free 50 hours can reproduce that post-training procedure. |
| [DINOv2](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07193v2), §6 and Appendix B | Curated 142M-image corpus; small encoder is distilled from the giant model. | Its masked patch objective raises ADE20k linear-probe mIoU from 44.2 to 47.1 in the reported ablation. | Supports retaining spatial patch information. The paper does not provide an effect size for continued DINOv2 training on 50 hours of a game followed by IDM/BC. This is a strong generic baseline, not proof adaptation will help. |
| [LAPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11758v1), Tables 1 and 16 | 60k Bridge trajectories, 970k Open-X trajectories, or 220k human Something-Something clips; real-world fine-tuning uses 150 labelled trajectories/task. | Open-X LAPA partial-credit score 50.09% versus OpenVLA 43.87% (+6.22); strict success 35.19% versus 27.78%. Human-video LAPA scores 34.02% versus scratch 21.22%. Fine grasping remains weaker. | Strong evidence for action-free pretraining helping low-label control, but the method trains a latent-action/VLA pipeline. Keeping only its encoder is an untested ablation, not the reported result. |
| [Genie](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391v1), §§3–4 and Appendix E | 30k filtered hours of 2D platformer footage from a >200k-hour collection; final world model 11B parameters. | CoinRun latent-action BC matches its oracle BC with as few as 200 expert action-mapping samples; numerical equality is the paper's reported finding, not a Rivals prediction. | Direct game evidence for useful latent actions, but not encoder-only initialization, continuous camera control, or a 50-hour training regime. |

A cheap temporal-order objective is also plausible: [Shuffle and Learn](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08561) demonstrates action-recognition transfer from ordering video frames. In our setting a countdown, progress bar, edit, or match timer could solve order without useful action understanding. Temporal contrastive objectives have the related risk of declaring two visually similar states “negative,” or forcing adjacent frames to be invariant precisely when their difference encodes a camera command. Use these as diagnostic alternatives, not the first headline result.

**Evidence judgment:** there is good precedent for representation pretraining aiding low-label control, and for in-domain video aiding recognition. There is no clean effect-size estimate for our exact combination: older-patch first-person/third-person game footage, a small encoder, continuous camera degrees, and about three paired hours. VPT's pseudo-labelled-video BC is a different mechanism and does not answer this pre-Gate-2 SSL question.

## 3. Method choice under the Mac and pixel constraints

| Candidate | Fit and main risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Cached DINOv2-small + small temporal feature predictor | Reuses pinned generic features, trains few parameters, no repeated image-backbone backward pass. Cannot recover information the frozen frame representation discarded. | First bounded pilot. Call it temporal-feature SSL, not reproduced V-JEPA. |
| Continued DINOv2-style image SSL | Architecture-compatible frame weights; potentially better world/HUD appearance. Tiny homogeneous video batches can forget broad features or learn patch/layout shortcuts; teacher/student, centering and patch losses need a real implementation. | Second choice if the first probe shows useful temporal information but inadequate spatial features. Not just “resume the feature extractor.” |
| VideoMAE-small / v2 dual masking | Strong standard video objective; approximately 22M backbone parameters for a small model, high masking saves encoder work. Pixel reconstruction may prioritize textures, overlays or camera motion; downstream dense video encoding is still costly. | Credible full-video follow-up; use matched generic-video initialization control as well as generic DINO. |
| V-JEPA / V-JEPA 2 as published | Latent prediction targets motion; published backbones/predictors and schedules are much larger than this pilot. Current public GPU recipes are not an MPS benchmark. | Borrow the objective, not the scale. No published-model pretraining campaign on this queue. |
| LAPA / Genie as encoder initializers | Extra quantizer, dynamics decoder and policy stages; latent codes can represent camera/effects rather than intended actions. Encoder-only usefulness is unmeasured. | No-go now. Do not assign semantic buttons or degrees to their latent indices. |
| Contrastive / order-only | Cheap, but time/layout shortcuts and inappropriate invariances are easy. | Possible diagnostic control if prediction fails; not a concurrent method sweep. |

**HUD resolution:** shrinking 2560 pixels to 224 makes a hypothetical 12-pixel-wide glyph only about one pixel wide. No SSL objective can recover digit information removed before encoding. Keep native HUD crops and their existing supervised/reader pathway separate from global-world pretraining. Preserve spatial patch grids instead of only CLS/global averaging. Do not randomly crop away the relevant HUD or flip left/right during camera supervision. Apply any geometric augmentation consistently across a clip; random per-frame jitter itself creates apparent camera motion. Native-resolution spot checks must verify that re-encoding and crops still preserve the needed marks before large extraction.

**Camera motion:** a video model can learn scene displacement without learning which displacement was commanded. Automatic turns, ability-driven motion, hero movement, FOV, perspective and camera shake remain confounders. Preserve the current 448-wide grey-difference camera path; do not replace it with an averaged semantic embedding. Downstream degrees continue to come from the paired logger/calibration contract. The inventory's loose statement that IDM uses 720p should not override the actual [model input contract](../../../policy/idm/model.py).

**Policy causality:** an offline IDM may inspect future frames; a live policy may not. Transfer per-frame weights or a causal temporal encoder. A bidirectional clip feature computed using future frames cannot be handed to the policy as a time-t observation. Historical `kit_context` also cannot be inferred at time t from future countdowns and then presented as a causal feature.

## 4. Concrete pilot: learn a temporal encoder without pseudo-actions

This is a proposed small architecture, not a published recipe with demonstrated Rivals performance:

1. Select one 16-frame clip per four seconds of admitted source. Half use 60 Hz samples, spanning .25 seconds; half use 8 Hz samples, spanning 1.875 seconds. Balance by session and keep true timestamps. The short clips target local motion; the longer clips expose animation and resource-transition context. No clip crosses a cut, gap or admission boundary.
2. Extract the pinned generic DINOv2-small's ordered CLS + 4×4 patch-grid features, **17×384 values/frame**, using the existing [frame feature convention](../../../policy/range_bc/cm3_features.py). Global input has the same layout in both downstream arms. For this world-motion pilot, mask the fixed HUD overlay regions consistently in every arm's DINO view; preserve the separate native HUD input. Pin geometry rather than changing masks per clip. This is a new cache identity, not an overwrite of round-3 features.
3. Train a causal temporal encoder: shared 384→192 token projection, fixed spatial and elapsed-time encodings, two Transformer blocks at width 192, four heads, FFN width 768. It has about **0.964M trainable parameters**, excluding readout adapters. Keep spatial cells separate. Within-frame attention is permitted; future-frame attention is not.
4. For SSL, feed the first eight frames and predict the fixed generic feature targets of the next eight with a small query decoder of roughly another 1M parameters. Use normalized-feature prediction error, with equal weight per spatial cell; no semantic actions, optical-flow labels, inferred camera degrees or reader events. Targets are stop-gradient, cached features. Discard the prediction decoder downstream. This is fixed-teacher predictive representation learning, not V-JEPA's EMA-teacher training.
5. Fix ten passes and seeds 0/1/2, with a preregistered optimizer recipe rather than selecting an epoch from downstream folds. A plausible starting recipe is AdamW, batch 16 clips, lr 1e-4, weight decay .01, gradient clip 1, 10% warmup then cosine decay. This is an engineering starting point, not an empirically selected optimum. Record error relative to copying the last frame's features; constant backgrounds make that a meaningful SSL baseline. Feature variance and moving/still residuals are diagnostics, not acceptance gates for control quality.

Because the image encoder remains frozen, the pilot cannot improve digit reading or spatial sensitivity inside that encoder. Its question is narrower and cheaper: **does predictive temporal initialization extract more useful control information from the same generic frame features?** It also avoids claiming that caching generic features alone constitutes pretraining.

The latter two alternatives are **not additive parts of the pilot** and are not full reproductions of the papers' long training schedules. Three seeds would triple their fit times. No claim of convergence after ten passes is made. The ranges are workload assumptions to turn into a smoke forecast, not extrapolations from Apple's peak FLOPs or NVIDIA timings.

**Decode can dominate.** A worst-case sequential pass through 1440p120 sources at an assumed 240–960 decoded source frames/s would take 1.25–5 hours for ten source hours, or 6.25–25 hours for fifty. At only 120 frames/s it takes the full source duration. Sparse selected frames do not imply equally sparse HEVC decoding because inter-frame dependencies and seeks cost work. Codec support and actual transfer speed must be measured. The archive is about 1.33 TB; transferring that entire archive is not a prerequisite. Pin selected finalized files and extract/cache once on the owner-approved machine path. Do not use a growing compressed output as an optimization.

**Memory estimates:** the ~1M-parameter temporal encoder plus predictor should fit comfortably in roughly 2–6 GB active MPS memory at modest batches, although attention/allocator overhead needs measurement. Frozen DINO extraction at the existing at-most-eight-view batch should be budgeted around 2–4 GB provisionally. A 22M-parameter ViT's weights, gradients and Adam state are about .35 GB in float32 before teacher, heads and activations; budget roughly 8–24 GB for small image/video SSL, not .35 GB total. A 1B-parameter model starts near 16 GB for those states alone, before large video activations. RAM capacity does not make its training schedule economical.

Use the installed PyTorch/MPS stack and verify the actual operators, numerical parity, precision and peak memory in a small future smoke. [PyTorch documents CPU fallback for unsupported MPS operations](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/mps_environment_variables.html); a run that silently falls back is not evidence for the estimated GPU throughput. No package or runtime change was made here.

## 6. The smallest decisive downstream comparison

There is an architecture mismatch to resolve explicitly: the existing camera encoder is a grey-difference CNN, not DINOv2. DINO/VideoMAE weights cannot simply initialize its convolutions. Preserve its input path and camera loss, and test an **additional feature branch** while retaining an unchanged-camera control.

### Data and arms

Use whole-session train-held-out folds **051828 and 200129**, with frozen dev 171533/205528, gate2, validation, test and sealed identities excluded everywhere. All three arms get identical paired examples, context eligibility, calibration and training budgets. Hold the named test session out of fitting and every SSL stage. Initially use the fixed five-session interim train population (80.53 minutes) for the bounded probe; confirm a positive result on the eight-session expanded allowed population (166.92 minutes). These populations are different experiments, and a changed conclusion must be reported.

The earlier 180.56-minute descriptive full admitted cohort includes the two forbidden frozen-dev sessions. It is **not** an allowed training population here. No new cohort statistic or action-support count was computed for this brief; do not import old rare-action support assumptions into either experiment.

| Arm | Frame and temporal representation | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| C | Existing CNN camera architecture, trained with the current accepted loss/uncertainty recipe | Establish current baseline on these exact train folds. Reuse only genuinely matched accepted runs. |

For F/U, gather a causal 16-frame dense feature history ending at the labelled interval end. Project the last-frame temporal summary to 64 dimensions and concatenate it with the CNN's existing 128-dimensional feature. Widen only the camera MLP's first input accordingly; retain yaw/pitch means, log-variance outputs, degree units and accepted loss/calibration protocol. Paired initialization copies the shared camera weights and initializes added columns identically; zero added columns give a useful initial equivalence check. Fine-tune the CNN, camera MLP and temporal encoder on paired data, keeping DINO frozen in both arms. Do not freeze a checkpoint already trained on the held-out session.

Three seeds per arm, paired with the three SSL seeds, two folds, three fixed epochs: **18 fits** if C needs rerunning; 12 if a genuinely identical C result exists. Use the same paired-training recipe and final-epoch rule, no held-out checkpoint selection. A two-arm F/U comparison is sufficient to identify an SSL gain; C determines whether the added branch is worth adopting at all. The tested temporal/history branch is candidate code, not a change to the accepted model during this research task.

### Camera and press decisions

Use the [IDM lane's existing measures](../../lanes/inverse-dynamics.md): per-axis moving-interval median absolute degree error, direction agreement, .25 s/1 s integrated error, uncertainty/risk–coverage, answered share, and fast-turn/swing/ability-camera strata. ## 7. Queue cost and decision to hand back

At a provisional 150–600 paired anchors/s, the twelve F/U camera fits above cost roughly **3.8–15.3 hours on the interim population** or **9.0–36.0 hours on expanded allowed data**, before prediction/calibration. Rerunning six C fits at the old .0029 s/example reference adds about **3.3 / 7.8 hours** respectively. These use nominal 60 Hz counts and three passes, with whole 051828/200129 sessions removed from each fit; actual context trimming reduces counts. The [prior handoff](press-head-design.md) records the source counts and historical timing reference.

For a ten-hour SSL source pilot, allow roughly **13–45 sequential Mac hours end to end** for the interim camera decision: .75–7.5 hours temporal pretraining, .33–1.33 hours SSL feature extraction, 1.25–5 hours source decode, roughly .7–2.7 hours paired DINO extraction, 7.1–18.6 hours camera fitting, plus approximately 3–10 hours for paired preparation, transfer/hash checks, numerical smoke and calibration/scoring. This excludes human source inspection and implementation. A slow decoder or CPU fallback can exceed the range. Expanded-cohort confirmation is roughly **25–76 hours if prepared and run independently**; cached SSL and source work can be reused when manifests and settings are unchanged. Press and policy experiments are additional queues, not included in the camera estimate.

**Go condition:** the owner can admit ten hours without compromising reserved data, and a smoke forecast fits a lead-selected queue slot. Recommend a **48-hour ceiling for the complete initial interim camera experiment**, including preparation and scoring, not a 48-hour permission to search methods. Stage all three arms/seeds on 051828 first; stop for futility if the predeclared criterion fails, and require 200129 before claiming the two-fold result. Do not shorten epochs or omit seeds to squeeze a failing forecast under the ceiling. Do not make this experiment a prerequisite that delays ready Gate 2 work.

**No-go now:** all-50-hour backbone training, published-scale V-JEPA 2, LAPA/Genie reproduction, or treating older footage as current-patch labelled control. Fifty hours of diversity might help, but duration alone neither establishes clean usable data nor justifies days of the Mac queue. If the cheap temporal pilot fails because frozen spatial features lack the relevant signal, a small VideoMAE or continued DINO arm remains a named follow-up uncertainty—not an automatic next run.

The decision available now is to prepare a bounded, train-only representation experiment after source and compute checks. The evidence does not support announcing that unlabelled pretraining has already improved either consumer, or that it bypasses the action-labelling and live-control gates.
